Out of the noise,
the jobs that fit you.

JobHunterX reads your resume, finds real openings on companies’ own job boards, and explains how well each one fits — and how likely a person is to read your application. Then it writes honest documents and helps you apply, while you watch.

The problem

Your application is overexposed.

A public post on a big job site can collect hundreds of applicants in days. Recruiters read the first few dozen and filter on years, current company and notice period. Early in your career, you’re often filtered out before anyone reads a word.

public listing · 3 weeks old · 300+ applied
Day 1AI Engineer · company’s own board
Too much light.
Big boards mix in other countries, other careers and other seniority levels. You sort them yourself, one by one.
Arriving late.
By the time a popular post reaches you, the crowd is there. The same role was often on the company’s own careers page first.
A number, no reasons.
“92% match” — and nothing about what fits, what’s missing, or why a job full of your keywords still won’t take you.
Retouched resumes.
Rewrite tools add skills you don’t have. The interview finds out.

“Apply to more” doesn’t work. Being seen early, on the right channel, does.

1Your resume

Upload once. Check what it understood.

JobHunterX reads your PDF and builds a profile: roles, skills and where you used them, your location. Years of experience are calculated from your dates — total, without internships, and internships alone. Nothing is guessed.

A “how we see you” screen shows the result, so you can fix anything before the first search.

3Real openings

Straight from the source: the company’s own board.

An employer job board is the careers page a company runs itself, usually through a hiring system like Greenhouse, Lever or Ashby. Posts there are first-hand and often appear before the big sites copy them.

Web search results are only leads. Every posting is checked: real, reachable, still open, in your country. Duplicates are merged; the employer’s copy wins.

While the app is open, it re-checks your watchlist companies every 4 hours. New roles land in a “New” tab.

4The measuring

Two readings, never mixed.

Fit asks “am I right for this job?”. Hard checks come first — career track, years, location, must-have skills, notice. A failed hard check caps the score, so keywords can’t lift a job you can’t get.

Chance to be seen asks “will a person read my application?” — from how fresh the post is, where it lives, and how crowded it looks.

Pick a frame. Candidate: ~1.5 years of AI work, Hyderabad, not relocating. These cases mirror an example in the project’s test suite; numbers are illustrative.

5Honest documents

Only what’s on your negative develops.

For a job you pick, it writes a one-page resume, a cover letter under 300 words and a CV of your whole career — five ATS-safe styles, PDF download.

Every AI-written line is fact-checked against your profile. A new number, tool, employer or claim is rejected and your own words stay. Skills the job wants that you lack are listed for you, never added.

6The browser agent

It fills the form. Your hand is always on the switch.

Press Auto-apply: documents are checked, missing ones written, then a private browser opens streamed inside the app, with every step in plain words.

Stop now stops at once. Take over pauses the agent and your clicks go to the page — for a CAPTCHA, login or anything else. Give back and it carries on from where you left it.

    7The tracker

    Every application, hung up to dry where you can see it.

    Six stages — Saved, Preparing, Applied, Interviewing, Offer, Closed — with one click to the next stage and a hint for what to do there.

    Log how you applied (company site, referral, a note to the team) and what happened. The Results view shows which routes actually get you interviews.

    Press “Next stage”. A tracker shows progress — it can’t promise an offer.

    Inside · every search, frame by frame

    Ten frames, one roll.

    What happens each time you press Search. Each frame names the part of the code that does it. Scroll sideways.

    01 · UNDERSTAND

    Read you properly

    Career tracks, realistic titles, skills with evidence. Years computed from dates. Every AI claim is checked against your profile.

    intelligence/candidate.py
    02 · PLAN

    Ask good questions

    Varied searches from your own titles and places, aimed at employers’ hiring systems.

    discovery/search.py
    03 · DISCOVER

    Go to the source

    Search results are leads. Employer boards and watchlist companies are read through their public APIs. Out-of-country jobs drop here.

    discovery/ats.py · watchlist.py
    04 · NORMALIZE

    One shape for all

    Every lead becomes one posting: hiring-system API first, then page data, then page text.

    discovery/page.py
    05 · DEDUPE

    Merge the copies

    The same job from many places becomes one. The first-hand source wins; all sources are kept.

    discovery/dedupe.py
    06 · VALIDATE

    Is it real?

    Real, reachable, current, open. Each field is verified, inferred, unverified or unknown — unknown stays unknown.

    discovery/validate.py
    07 · EXTRACT

    Read the post

    Required vs nice-to-have skills, years, notice, salary. Numbers are accepted only with a quote from the post.

    intelligence/job.py
    08 · MATCH

    Hard checks first

    Track, years, seniority, location, must-haves, notice, salary. A failed one caps the score.

    intelligence/matching.py
    09 · RANK & EXPLAIN

    Say why

    0–100 score and verdict, strengths, gaps, unknowns — plus the separate chance to be seen.

    intelligence/reach.py
    10 · CONNECT

    Reach a person

    For top matches: an email the post itself gives (never guessed), recruiter and alumni search links you choose from, a short note in your voice.

    intelligence/connect.py
    From the README’s “10 stages of every search”.
    Agent · Scout

    Finds

    Runs the searches and reads company job boards — in the app it heads for the web or the “server room” of company boards.

    Agent · Verifier

    Checks

    Stamps a posting as real and open, or bins it — closed, unreachable, or not in your country.

    Agent · Analyst

    Measures

    Reads each post and scores the fit — and gets visibly excited about a strong one.

    Agent · Connector

    Reaches out

    Finds a way to a real person for your top matches and drafts a short note — paper planes, in the app’s animation.

    In the app, Discover → Agents shows nine agents as characters in a 3D office. Every work scene comes from a real event of your running search; break time never claims progress.

    Use cases · example situations

    Who it helps, and how.

    Four everyday situations, each matched to features the app really has. The people are examples.

    Fresher · 0 years · Hyderabad

    “Every post says 1–3 years. Do I even apply?”

    • Experience bands instead of a hard cap: within reach, apply with a referral, stretch, out of range.
    • A separate safety-net tab for large IT-services fresher programmes.
    • A scam filter for fees, UPI, WhatsApp-only contact, OTP requests and weeks-old websites.
    Two years in · can’t relocate

    “I want to be early, not applicant #1,500.”

    • A watchlist of about 130 researched companies, checked every 4 hours while the app is open.
    • Chance to be seen beside every fit score.
    • Notice-period aware: “immediate joiners only” is flagged when yours is long.
    Found a role elsewhere

    “I already have the link.”

    • Paste a job description or link and get a resume, CV, cover letter — or all three.
    • Paste a company job link and the agent applies; watch it or take over.
    Wants a person, not a portal

    “Who do I actually talk to?”

    • The Connector gives the application email a post itself lists — never a guessed one.
    • Search links for recruiters, the hiring team and alumni of your college; you choose the person, nothing is scraped.
    • A referral pack: job link and ID, three lines to paste, one proof link.

      Principles

      What it won’t do.

      Not a mass-application bot. Some lines it doesn’t cross.

      1. Invent a skill, number or employer.

        Generated text is checked against your profile. Anything new is rejected.

      2. Round your experience up.

        Years come from your dates; the agent answers knock-out questions truthfully.

      3. Spray your resume everywhere.

        Auto-apply runs on the job you choose. Fewer, better-fitted applications.

      4. Guess emails or scrape people.

        Only an email the post gives, and search links where you pick the person.

      5. Fake progress.

        Progress bars move with real counts the server reports — never estimated from time.

      Run from source

      Two lines, then one command.

      Never used a terminal? Fine. Copy, paste, press Enter. A script does the rest.

      Your computer
      You need
      • Internet for the first setup.
      • Git — or download the ZIP.
      • Google Chrome, used by Auto-apply.
      • One free AI key — or none, with “Start free”.

      No need to install Python: the script gets Python 3.11 through uv.

      Repository
      The project’s code, kept on GitHub.
      Clone
      Copy that code to your computer.
      Terminal
      A window where you type commands. On Windows: PowerShell.
      AI API key
      A password-like code that lets the app use an AI service. Keep it private.

      1. Open a terminal

        Press Start, type PowerShell, open it.

        Press ⌘ Space, type Terminal, press Enter.

        Open your terminal — often Ctrl Alt T.

      2. Copy the project, step inside

        Downloads the code into a folder named jobhunterx.

        PS> git clone https://github.com/kvcops/jobhunterx.git
PS> cd jobhunterx

      3. Start it

        PS> powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File start.ps1

        Or double-click start.bat in the folder. -ExecutionPolicy Bypass applies to this one run only.

        $ bash start.sh

      4. First run sets itself up

        It installs uv, makes a private environment, installs packages (about 30 seconds), creates the settings file, checks everything loads, and opens the app in your browser once it answers.

      5. Add a free AI key — or don’t

        The first screen shows where to get a free key, tests it with one call and saves it. Google AI Studio is easiest; NVIDIA, Groq and Mistral work too. Two or three keys make searches faster.

        No key? Start free uses Kilo’s free models — marked as possibly training on your prompts, which include your resume text.

      6. Use, stop, restart

        The app lives at http://127.0.0.1:9000 — on your own computer. Stop: Ctrl + C in the terminal. Next time, run the same start command.

      Before you start

      Privacy, needs, honest limits.

      Where your data lives

      • The desktop app keeps data in its own folder on your computer.
      • From source: a local SQLite database, keys in a local .env that git ignores.
      • No JobHunterX account or cloud. In optional multi-user server mode, users’ keys are stored encrypted.

      What leaves your computer

      • Resume and job-post text go to the AI provider you choose — Google, NVIDIA, Groq, Mistral or Kilo.
      • Kilo’s free models may train on prompts. Prefer privacy? Add any free key and turn Kilo off in Settings → AI providers.
      • Searching and applying visit public job sites, like a browser does.

      Limits

      • Free AI plans allow a few calls a minute; one key can be slow.
      • CAPTCHAs, logins and codes may need you — that’s Take over.
      • Installers aren’t code-signed or notarized yet. macOS: Apple Silicon only. Linux: x86-64.
      • No tool can promise interviews.

      Who it’s for

      Job seekers in India — jobs outside your country are dropped before scoring. The researched watchlist covers Hyderabad, Bengaluru and remote India; the README says more South Indian cities come next. Sharpest for 0–3 years of experience.

      Questions

      Asked & answered.

      What exactly is JobHunterX?
      An app on your computer that helps with the whole job search: understands your resume and goals, finds real openings, explains fit and chance to be seen, writes honest documents, fills applications with a browser agent you can take over, and tracks everything.
      Is it free?
      Yes — open source under the MIT license. It uses AI providers’ free tiers: a free key, or Kilo’s free models with no key. Provider limits can change.
      Is the source code available?
      All of it, on GitHub. Read it, run it, fork it, contribute.
      Does it apply to every job automatically?
      No. Searching runs on its own; Auto-apply starts when you press it on a job you chose. You watch live and can stop, take over, or finish and mark it submitted yourself.
      Can I review an application first?
      Yes. Documents are ready to read and edit before the browser opens, and every agent step is shown. Take over any time.
      What about logins and CAPTCHAs?
      The application shows “needs you”. Take over, click and type in the live view, press Continue. Progress is saved — even across an app restart.
      Do I need an AI API key?
      Not strictly — “Start free” works with no key. A free Google AI Studio key is the easy private option.
      Where is my data stored?
      On your computer. Text the AI must read goes to the provider you choose.
      Windows, macOS, Linux?
      Installers: Windows 10/11 x64, macOS 11+ Apple Silicon, Linux x86-64 (AppImage, .deb, .rpm). Other machines can run from source.
      How is it different from job alerts?
      Alerts forward keywords. JobHunterX checks a post is real and open, reads it, applies hard checks, explains the score, estimates your chance of being seen, and goes to employers’ own boards first.
      Does it guarantee interviews?
      No. It helps you send fewer, better-fitted applications, earlier, with honest documents.
      Does it run locally?
      Yes, on 127.0.0.1, unless you choose the optional multi-user server mode. It needs the internet to search and reach AI providers.
